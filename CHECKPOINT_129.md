# Checkpoint 129 - Determinant as Area Scale Factor in R^2

## Purpose

Develop the first precise geometric meaning of determinant magnitude:

\[
|\det(A)|=\text{area scale factor}.
\]

The sign of the determinant is deliberately deferred to CP130.

## Mathematical narrative

1. Begin with the unit square, whose area is 1.
2. Display the standard basis vectors \(\mathbf e_1\) and \(\mathbf e_2\).
3. Apply
   \[
   A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}.
   \]
4. Transform the basis vectors into the matrix columns
   \(\mathbf a_1=(2,0)^T\) and \(\mathbf a_2=(1,2)^T\).
5. Transform the unit square into the parallelogram generated by those columns.
6. Make the base and perpendicular height visible and calculate its area as \(2\cdot2=4\).
7. Compare original area 1 with image area 4, then state that the area scale factor is 4.
8. Conclude with \(|\det(A)|=\text{area scale factor}\), while previewing that the sign will be explained next.

## Architecture decisions

- The renderer-independent engine computes transformed vertices and polygon area.
- The example uses integer columns and a visually transparent base-height calculation.
- The same coordinate system remains fixed throughout.
- The original square is transformed in place; no persistent reference copy remains behind.
- The lesson treats determinant magnitude only. Orientation belongs to CP130.
- The render script explicitly places the repository root on `PYTHONPATH`.
